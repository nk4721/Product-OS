# Vanthom Intelligence — Discovery Plan

## Discovery decision

Determine whether Vanthom Intelligence addresses a recurring and commercially meaningful decision problem, and identify the narrowest responsible use case for a paid pilot.

## Research boundaries

Discovery must test the problem before promoting the solution. Interviews should not assume that an AI assistant, shared data layer or autonomous agent is the answer.

Customer-specific information and licensed supplier data must not be copied into this repository.

## Core hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Evidence needed | Disconfirming signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Leaders lose meaningful time gathering context before recurring decisions. | Recent examples, frequency and time/cost estimate. | The task is rare, fast or already well supported. |
| H2 | Poor traceability creates rework, risk or low trust. | Examples of challenged decisions, missing evidence or repeated analysis. | Users value speed only and do not need source transparency. |
| H3 | One coherent segment shares a repeatable decision workflow. | Similar triggers, inputs, roles and outputs across organisations. | Every case requires bespoke consulting and unrelated data. |
| H4 | Users will allow an AI workflow to analyse approved sources when controls are clear. | Security and permission requirements plus willingness to pilot. | Required data cannot be processed or trust barriers remain unacceptable. |
| H5 | An economic buyer will fund a measurable pilot. | Named buyer, budget logic and outcome threshold. | Interest exists but no owner, budget or measurable value. |

## Initial participant profiles

Recruit participants with recent responsibility for decisions based on several information sources:

- product leaders and product managers
- commercial or operational leaders
- CRM and customer-success leaders
- consultants producing analysis and recommendations
- security, privacy or IT stakeholders involved in approval

Avoid recruiting only AI enthusiasts; include users who are sceptical or satisfied with current methods.

## Interview guide

Ask for a recent real example before discussing Vanthom Intelligence.

1. What decision were you trying to make?
2. What triggered the work, and how often does it happen?
3. Who needed the outcome and who was accountable?
4. Which information sources were required?
5. What did you have to do manually?
6. Where did uncertainty, disagreement or delay occur?
7. What happened when information was missing or wrong?
8. How did you document evidence, assumptions and the final decision?
9. What is the business consequence of the current process?
10. What tools, analysts or consultants do you use today?
11. What would need to be true before AI could support this workflow?
12. Which information must never be combined, stored or exposed?
13. How would you measure a successful improvement?
14. Who would approve and pay for a pilot?

Do not lead with feature lists. Show the concept only after the current workflow, consequences and alternatives are understood.

## Evidence capture

For each observation, record:

- participant role and segment
- decision workflow
- trigger and frequency
- current steps and sources
- time, cost, risk or missed opportunity
- current alternative
- direct evidence
- researcher interpretation
- unresolved assumption
- security, privacy and contractual constraints
- buying role and pilot interest

Keep direct evidence separate from interpretation.

## Validation activities

### 1. Problem interviews

Test whether the same costly decision workflow recurs across a coherent segment.

**Proceed signal:** repeated recent examples with similar triggers, inputs and consequences.  
**Stop or pivot signal:** general enthusiasm without a concrete recurring problem.

### 2. Concierge decision brief

With explicit permission, manually create a source-linked decision brief for one narrow workflow. Measure preparation time, usefulness, trust, corrections and whether the output changes or accelerates a decision.

No production integration is required for this test.

### 3. Prototype comprehension test

Test a low-fidelity flow containing:

- decision question
- approved sources
- facts and assumptions
- uncertainty or conflict
- recommendation and options
- human approval
- owner and next action

Measure whether users understand where claims come from and what requires judgement.

### 4. Paid-pilot conversation

Present a bounded pilot with a measurable outcome, limited data scope, named responsibilities and a fixed review point. Test commitment, not only stated interest.

## Proposed pilot criteria

A responsible first pilot should have:

- one recurring decision workflow
- one accountable customer owner
- explicitly approved data sources
- no silent cross-supplier data substitution
- human review of every material recommendation
- agreed baseline and outcome metric
- documented retention and deletion rules
- a clear end-of-pilot proceed, pivot or stop decision

## Initial outcome metrics

Select only those relevant to the validated workflow:

- preparation time per decision
- elapsed time from question to decision
- percentage of claims linked to approved evidence
- number and severity of corrections
- user-rated confidence with explanation
- action completion after a decision
- willingness to continue or pay after the pilot

Raw prompt count and message volume are not customer-value metrics.

## Decision rules

**Proceed** when one segment demonstrates a recurring costly problem, a responsible data path, a measurable pilot outcome and commercial commitment.

**Pivot** when the problem is real but the segment, workflow or value proposition differs from the current hypothesis.

**Park** when value exists but access, timing, buyer ownership or operational readiness is insufficient.

**Stop** when the problem is weak, bespoke, already solved or cannot be addressed within acceptable data and accountability constraints.

## Immediate next actions

1. Select two candidate decision workflows to investigate.
2. Recruit participants across users, buyers and governance stakeholders.
3. Run problem interviews and maintain an evidence log.
4. Choose at most one workflow for a concierge test.
5. Review evidence with the Product Director.
6. Record a proceed, pivot, park or stop decision before implementation.
